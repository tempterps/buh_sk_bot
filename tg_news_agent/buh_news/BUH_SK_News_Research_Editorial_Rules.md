# BUH.SK — News Research & Editorial Rules

**Consolidated editorial policy · Version 1.1 · 28 November 2026**

These rules define **what news to look for, how to select it, what makes it valuable to BUH.SK readers, and how to write articles and Telegram posts**.

## 1. Our audience and editorial objective

BUH.SK provides accounting, tax, company registration and related administrative services in Slovakia.

Our primary audience consists of Ukrainian-speaking people living or doing business in Slovakia, especially owners of **živnosť and s.r.o.**, including businesses with employees and entrepreneurs who need Slovak residence permits.

Every publication should ideally achieve three objectives: readers recognise that the information affects them personally, find it valuable enough to share with someone, and associate BUH.SK with competent professional support.

**We are not trying to become a general news outlet.** We are building a source of information that entrepreneurs would otherwise overlook, misunderstand or discover too late.

A day without a worthwhile publication is better than a day with irrelevant content.

## 2. Where to search for information

### Primary official sources — highest priority

| Source | Information to monitor |

| [Slovensko.sk](https://www.slovensko.sk/) | Electronic government services and business obligations |

### Analytical sources — search for surprising findings

| Source | Information to monitor |
|---|---|
| [Národná banka Slovenska](https://nbs.sk/) | SME lending, financial conditions, bank practices, business risks |
| [FinStat](https://www.finstat.sk/) | Business performance, bankruptcies, debt trends, sector analyses |
| [CRIF](https://www.crif.sk/) | Company solvency, insolvencies, liquidations, payment discipline |

| [Slovak Business Agency](https://www.sbagency.sk/) | Research on SMEs and business conditions |

### Business media — discovery, not final authority

Use [Podnikajte.sk](https://www.podnikajte.sk/), [Trend](https://www.trend.sk/), [Hospodárske noviny](https://hnonline.sk/), [Aktuality.sk](https://www.aktuality.sk/), [Denník E](https://e.dennikn.sk/) and [Euractiv.sk](https://euractiv.sk/) to discover stories.

A media report should lead to investigation of the underlying official announcement, legislation or research, not simply be rewritten.

### Specialist sources

Also search Slovak health insurance companies, [IOM Migration Information Centre](https://www.mic.iom.sk/), [ÚPSVaR](https://www.upsvr.gov.sk/), [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/), [European Commission tax policy](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/), [MIRRI](https://mirri.gov.sk/) and [SARIO](https://www.sario.sk/).

EU developments matter when they create a reasonably concrete consequence for Slovak entrepreneurs. Grants are interesting only when the relevant audience is actually eligible.

## 3. What the researcher should look for

The researcher should not simply search for the word *news*. It should look for **unexpected obligations, hidden financial consequences, contradictions between common assumptions and actual rules, and genuinely useful findings**.

### Unexpected obligations

Something an entrepreneur must do even when they believe it does not apply to them. For example, an inactive s.r.o. facing administrative obligations, or a non-DPH entrepreneur affected by an electronic invoicing requirement.

### Money they may lose or unexpectedly pay

Increased contributions, missed deductions, additional taxes, penalties, compliance expenses or changes in the real cost of operating a business.

### Consequences beyond accounting

Debts affecting business reputation, tax problems complicating business-based residence, administrative failures disrupting contracts, or financial issues affecting credit decisions.

### Surprising research and real cases

Unexpected statistics, findings from NBS or company research, real tax inspections, common mistakes and overlooked exemptions that may apply to readers.

These are topic categories, not permission to invent dramatic consequences.

## 4. What our readers are most likely to find interesting

The strongest content creates one of these reactions:

- **“I didn't know that.”** A real obligation or exception they were unaware of.
- **“I have exactly this situation.”** The reader recognises their own business, residence or financial circumstances.
- **“I need to send this to someone.”** The content is relevant to a specific friend, colleague or business owner.
- **“This could cost me money.”** The financial or administrative consequences are tangible.
- **“I should discuss this with my accountant.”** The story demonstrates the need for qualified professional support.

The best topics often combine several reactions.

### Examples from our experience

| Topic | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Živnosť contributions rising by €321/year | Direct, measurable financial impact |
| eFaktúra obligations for inactive s.r.o. | Unexpected obligation even without business activity |
| Public debtor list starting at €5 | Surprising threshold, reputation and possible residence implications |
| Payments received outside eKasa records | Recognisable accounting risk and financial consequences |
| A new requirement for employers | Readers with employees can immediately identify themselves |
| Unexpected exceptions in DPH rules | Corrects a common assumption and can save money |

The topic **“Tax inspectors discovered €36,000 of unpaid tax at another company”** is inherently weaker than **“You can have income outside eKasa that still needs to be accounted for.”**

The first describes somebody else's mistake. The second invites readers to consider their own situation.

## 5. Topics to avoid

Do not select material merely because it is recent, politically prominent or widely discussed.

Avoid generic economic commentary without a clear consequence for the target audience; repetitive tax reminders everyone already knows; company press releases announcing routine activities; sensational stories about very large corporations without a meaningful connection to Slovak SMEs; vague warnings about future legislation; and general investment or stock-market commentary unrelated to operating a business.

Do not repeatedly cover the same regulation unless there is a **new substantive development, clarification, deadline or exception**.

Do not publish grant announcements until basic eligibility is established.

Do not turn a weak article into a strong-looking story by exaggerating financial consequences or inventing a connection to residence permits, taxes or liability.

## 6. Ranking system — 10 points

Each candidate receives a score of 0, 1 or 2 in five dimensions.

| Dimension | Question | Score |
|---|---|---|
| Personal relevance | Does it apply to a common situation among BUH.SK readers? | 0–2 |
| Financial or practical impact | Does it affect money, obligations, deadlines or meaningful business risk? | 0–2 |
| Surprise factor | Is it genuinely non-obvious or counter to a common assumption? | 0–2 |
| Shareability | Is there an identifiable person who would benefit from receiving it? | 0–2 |
| BUH.SK service relevance | Does it create a natural reason to seek professional accounting or administrative help? | 0–2 |

**Thresholds:**

- **0–7/10:** Reject or reconsider the angle.
- **8/10:** A promising topic deserving editorial review, not automatic publication.
- **9–10/10:** A strong candidate generally worth drafting as a website article and Telegram post, subject to factual verification.

High scores never override factual accuracy, relevance or editorial approval.

## 7. Mandatory rejection rules

Regardless of its score, a story must not be recommended as verified content when it has no credible source, concerns a law that does not apply to Slovakia, confuses a proposal with a law already in force, misrepresents a figure or penalty, or substantially duplicates recently published content.

Claims with important unresolved uncertainty should be researched further, not written as established facts.

The researcher must distinguish the date of an announcement, the effective date of the rule, the transitional period and the date the underlying event occurred.

For example, an announcement updating a debtor list is **not necessarily a new law introducing that threshold**.

## 8. Telegram post — required content

The Telegram post must provide the actual value of the story inside Telegram, even when there is a longer article on buh.sk.

It should normally contain a short, compelling headline or opening; the unexpected fact; who it affects; the most meaningful consequence; and, where relevant, a natural invitation to contact BUH.SK.

**Length:** aim for one mobile screen. Approximately 500–900 characters is a useful editorial target, not a rigid requirement. Clarity matters more than exact character count.

Start with the strongest information. Avoid introductory paragraphs repeating who issued the announcement if they do not contribute to the hook.

Use **one main idea per post**.

### Headlines

Good headline:

> **Борг лише 5 € — і ваша компанія вже може потрапити до публічного списку боржників.**

Weak headline:

> Sociálna poisťovňa опублікувала оновлення списку боржників.

The first is concrete, surprising and personally meaningful. The second describes an administrative event.

### Endings and calls to action

Use a CTA related to the subject of the post.

For example:

> Маєте надходження на особисту картку й не впевнені, чи потрібно їх декларувати? Напишіть @buh_sk — розберемо вашу ситуацію.

A generic footer such as “Якщо потрібен бухгалтер — пишіть нам” is not forbidden, but it should not be automatically appended to every post, especially when it duplicates a stronger thematic CTA.

## 9. What NOT to include in Telegram

Do not overload posts with the full text of legislation, lists of article numbers, lengthy legal disclaimers, several unrelated examples, procedural details, excessive history or background.

Do not produce an empty teaser that says only “We found interesting news — read more on our website.” The reader should learn something valuable without clicking.

Do not use fear as the only reason to read. We prefer a surprising insight to an intimidating penalty.

Do not name third-party tools or commercial databases that show readers how to perform professional checks independently. For example, when discussing debts and company reputation, explain the risk without directing readers to FinStat or other platforms to perform the investigation themselves.

Do not include decorative emoji. Serious posts should generally use **zero emoji**. Where genuine humour makes them appropriate, use no more than three.

Do not change user-authored wording when asked only to format it for Telegram. Formatting means line breaks and emphasis, not rewriting.

## 10. Website articles — what to include

Website articles may be longer and more detailed than Telegram posts. They should demonstrate BUH.SK's competence and lead naturally to a service inquiry.

The preferred structure is:

1. **Problem affecting the reader.**
2. **Verified facts or real example.**
3. **Financial, legal or practical consequences.**
4. **Responsibility and the value of professional oversight.**
5. **Relevant BUH.SK service and CTA.**

Explain who the rule applies to, the relevant dates, exceptions and consequences. Where helpful, explain what a real tax case demonstrates.

The article should make readers understand **why the issue matters and why they may need qualified assistance**.

It should not become a detailed training manual for doing the accountant's work independently.

### What not to include on the website

Avoid step-by-step instructions for reconciling bank transactions, preparing tax returns, checking DPH deductions, auditing one's accounting records or handling other professional processes that BUH.SK provides as paid services.

Avoid long “What to check yourself” or “DIY checklist” sections that enable readers to replace the professional service.

Do not list competitors or third-party tools as self-service alternatives when they are unnecessary for understanding the issue.

However, do not withhold legally necessary information, exclusions or material facts simply to encourage inquiries. The article must remain genuinely informative, accurate and trustworthy.

## 11. Accuracy and responsibility

All legal, financial and regulatory claims must be supported by reliable evidence, preferably the official authority or legislation.

Never confuse a tax assessment with a penalty, a denied DPH deduction with a fine, or the value of a transaction with the amount of additional tax.

When describing bank account income, do not imply that every incoming transfer is taxable, or that a business bank-account case automatically proves an issue with a personal payment card.

When writing about residence permits, identify the relevant permit category. Do not claim that a €5 debt automatically cancels every type of Slovak residence.

When discussing commercial ratings, it is acceptable to explain that a public debt can affect reputation or an assessment of reliability. Do not claim that every €5 debt produces a fixed automatic rating reduction.

BUH.SK can help with accounting, compliance and risk identification, but should not promise that all fines will be avoided or that the accountant assumes the entrepreneur's statutory legal responsibility.

## 12. Language and tone

Telegram posts for the Ukrainian channel must be written **in Ukrainian**.

The tone should be direct, conversational, practical and occasionally dryly humorous. Avoid bureaucratic language, generic introductions, artificial enthusiasm and promotional clichés.

Expressions such as “Якщо ще не чули”, “Ну і...” and witty everyday comparisons can be natural, but they must not be inserted mechanically.

Use real figures and specific Slovak terminology where appropriate: živnosť, s.r.o., DPH, eKasa, eFaktúra.

For website content, Ukrainian and English versions should retain the same verified facts and legal distinctions, while reading naturally in their respective languages.

## 13. Publication principles

A good idea is not an approved post. A completed article is not automatically approved for the website, and approval of the website article does not authorise Telegram publication.

**Every publication requires explicit approval for that channel and its final content.**

No daily publishing quota should be imposed. News selection must be based on quality.

Measure engagement using posts of comparable age, especially reactions and forwarding rates. Also pay attention to website visits and real inquiries, because ultimately the purpose of BUH.SK content is to build trust and generate suitable clients.

---

## The single most important editorial rule

> Find information that makes a Slovak entrepreneur think: **“I didn't know this affected me. This could matter to my money, business or residence status. I should speak to someone competent.”**

Not just news. Not just penalties. Not instructions for doing their own accounting. **Unexpected, verified and personally relevant information that demonstrates the value of BUH.SK.**
